How it works

Four small steps, one settled receipt

Nothing to set up on the first receipt. The parts that cost money run last.

1

Snap or paste

Drop a photo, use the camera, or paste the raw text. The lines, prices and discounts are read for you.

2

Name the people

Add everyone who shared the basket. You are already on the list.

3

Assign the lines

Tick a few lines and tap a name, or open a line and split it evenly, by percentage, or by exact amount.

4

Settle and save

Tax lands in proportion, totals reconcile to the cent, and the receipt files itself into your history.

What it does

Built for the messy middle of a shared bill

The parts of splitting a receipt that usually mean a group chat and a calculator.

Reads the receipt for you

A photo goes to Gemini once and comes back as items, prices, discounts and totals. Paste text or JSON if you would rather.

Three ways to split a line

Evenly between whoever shared it, by percentage, or by exact amounts. Every line can use a different rule.

Batch select

Tick a run of lines, then one tap puts a person on all of them. The same bar marks food, ignores lines, or identifies just those.

Real names for shorthand

“GV SHRD MOZZ 8Z” becomes a product you recognise weeks later, with a confidence score and the reasoning behind it.

Tax, distributed fairly

Enter the tax once and it spreads in proportion to each person's subtotal. The summary tells you when the split balances.

Bank imports and a budget ring

Watch a monthly ring of spend by category. Admin accounts can also link a bank read-only through Plaid and attach receipts to transactions.

Item identification

Cheapest answer first. Never a made-up one.

Receipt shorthand is ambiguous, so naming runs in tiers. Each tier only sees what the one before it could not place, and the model is the last resort, not the first.

  • Corrections compound. Confirm a name once and the next receipt from that shop is named for free.
  • Item codes when the receipt prints them. A barcode number is unambiguous in a way “SHRD MOZZ” never is.
  • Confidence only when it matters. No chip on a name you confirmed or one the app is sure about.
  • Nothing invented. A line the tiers cannot place is reported as unidentified, with the runners-up kept as alternatives.

Yours to keep

Runs on your own ground

Keys stay on the server

Your Gemini key is encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM and never sent back to the browser.

Read-only bank access

Plaid tokens are exchanged server-side and stored encrypted, and only admin accounts can link a bank at all. Skip it entirely if you like.

Postgres you control

A Docker database locally, or point one connection string at Neon, Supabase or RDS.

Made for the checkout line

Capture from your phone camera and split on the spot. The layout is mobile-first.

Questions

Before you sign up

Do I need my own Gemini API key?

Admin accounts can use the shared key from the server's environment. Everyone else adds their own in Settings, where it is stored encrypted and used only for their receipts. The app only ever reports whether a key exists, never the key itself.

Is connecting my bank safe?

Bank links go through Plaid Link and are read-only. Access tokens are exchanged on the server and encrypted at rest, so they never reach your browser. Only admin accounts can link a bank, and everything else works without one.

What if an item is named wrong?

Click the item. You will see what the receipt printed, its code, brand, size, where the answer came from and why. Correct it, or pick one of the alternatives. The correction is remembered for that store.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. Take the photo straight from the camera, assign lines with your thumb, and the summary follows you down the page. The same account works on your laptop.

Where does my data live?

In a Postgres database you run. Locally that is a Docker container; to scale, point the connection string at a managed Postgres and apply the migrations. No code changes required.
